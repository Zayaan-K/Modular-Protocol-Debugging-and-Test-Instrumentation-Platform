1. Choose the fabrication stack-up

Select the PCB manufacturer you are most likely to use and examine its documented stack-ups and controlled-impedance capabilities.

USB 2.0 PCB Stack-up and Differential-Pair Geometry

Manufacturer:
JLCPCB

Board construction:
- Board type: Rigid FR-4
- Layer count: 4
- Nominal board thickness: 1.6 mm
- Selected stack-up: JLC04161H-3313A
- Finished stack-up thickness: 1.58 mm ±10%
- Outer copper weight: 1 oz
- Inner copper weight: 0.5 oz

Layer usage:
- L1: Components and USB D+/D− routing
- L2: Continuous ground reference plane
- L3: Power and low-speed signals
- L4: Low-speed signals and optional ground fill

USB differential-pair requirements:
- Target differential impedance: 90 Ω
- Routing type: Differential Pair (Non-coplanar)
- Signal layer: L1
- Reference layer: L2
- D+/D− trace width: 11.44 mil (0.2906 mm)
- D+/D− edge-to-edge gap: 8 mil (0.2032 mm)
- Impedance tolerance: Use JLCPCB-supported controlled-impedance tolerance

L1-to-L2 construction:
- L1 copper thickness: 0.0350 mm
- First 3313 prepreg thickness: 0.1070 mm
- Second 3313 prepreg thickness: 0.0994 mm
- Total L1-to-L2 dielectric thickness: 0.2064 mm
- L2 copper thickness: 0.0152 mm

Manufacturing requirements:
- Enable controlled impedance when ordering.
- Select the JLC04161H-3313A stack-up.
- Identify the D+/D− pair as a 90 Ω differential-impedance pair.
- Do not substitute another stack-up without recalculating the trace geometry.

Design notes:
- Route D+ and D− on L1 above an uninterrupted L2 ground plane.
- Maintain the specified width and spacing throughout the pair where practical.
- Avoid plane gaps, unnecessary vias, branches and long test-point stubs.
- Recalculate the geometry if the manufacturer, stack-up, copper weight,
  layer assignment or board thickness changes.

2. Create a simple rectangular Revision 1 outline. Keep it only as large as necessary for:

USB Type-A male connector
USB Type-A female connector
Base-board connector J3
Protection and link components
VBUS/GND probe access
Mounting or retention features

Check connector overhangs in the footprint courtyard and 3D viewer. USB shells must reach the board edge properly, and the male connector must have enough clearance to enter a computer or extension cable.

Place them in this order:

USB male and female connectors
D+/D− ESD array beside the exposed female connector
D+/D− 0 Ω links
Small optional probe pads
VBUS protection and measurement parts
Base-board connector J3
Mounting holes and labels

Aim for a direct physical corridor:

USB male ───────── D+/D− path ── ESD ── USB female

Keep J3 and slow fault-control lines outside that corridor. Do not force the USB pair through J3—the main D+/D− path should remain local to the interface module, with only control or measurement provisions going to the base board.


Placement rules:

ESD array extremely close to the female connector
No long branch before the ESD device
D+ and D− components aligned symmetrically
Minimal rotation changes
Enough space to route the pair without vias
Continuous L2 ground beneath the entire path
VBUS/GND test points accessible at the board edge
No mounting holes or plane cutouts beneath the pair


3. Configure KiCad rules and inspect

Create a USB differential-pair net class for D+ and D−. Enter the preliminary width and gap obtained from your chosen stack-up, but mark them as provisional until verified by the fabricator.

Configure:

Differential-pair width and gap
Copper clearance
Via dimensions
Board-edge clearance
Differential-pair skew constraint
Appropriate minimum drill sizes

Update the PCB from the schematic, inspect the unrouted ratsnest and use the 3D viewer to check connector orientation and mechanical interference.

Do not route D+/D− today unless the placement is clearly settled.
