finalize USB 2.0 design rules and begin the pass-through schematic

1. Record answers to:

Why D+ and D− are routed as a 90 Ω differential pair
Why they require a continuous ground reference
How branches, large test pads and vias create impedance discontinuities
Why ESD-device capacitance loads the data lines
Why the ESD device belongs close to the exposed connector

Write a short usb2-design-notes.md containing these preliminary rules:

Target 90 Ω differential impedance; final geometry determined from the fabricator’s stack-up.
Keep D+/D− short, parallel and on one layer where practical.
Maintain constant width, spacing and ground reference.
Avoid routing over plane gaps.
Avoid unnecessary vias and branches.
Put test pads directly in the route, not at the end of long traces.
Use a two-channel ESD device explicitly rated for USB 2.0 High-Speed.
Route through or immediately beside the ESD device with a short ground connection.

Do not attempt to calculate final trace dimensions, need to choose a PCB stack-up first.

2. Select the physical USB connection 

Computer/host → USB Type-A male → board → USB Type-A female → peripheral

Select exact connector part numbers that:

Are currently obtainable
Have datasheets with clear pin numbering
Include shield/mounting contacts
Have available or easily verifiable KiCad footprints
Use through-hole mounting tabs if possible for mechanical strength

Record the manufacturer part number, DigiKey/Mouser number and datasheet link.

3. Build the core schematic

Create the four continuous paths:

VBUS_HOST → VBUS_DEVICE
D-_HOST   → D-_DEVICE
D+_HOST   → D+_DEVICE
GND_HOST  → GND_DEVICE

Add:

One test point each for VBUS, D−, D+ and GND
One 0 Ω series-link position on VBUS, D− and D+
A placeholder for a two-channel USB 2.0 ESD array
Distinct connector-shield pins
Clear host-side and device-side net labels

Place the ESD array near the device-facing connector in the schematic structure, because that is the connector most likely to be touched during normal use.

For this passive revision, do not add:

Data-line MOSFET switches
Fault resistors or capacitors
External VBUS injection
A microcontroller or FPGA
Protocol decoding
USB-C circuitry

4. Verfiy

Check every connector pin against its datasheet and run KiCad ERC.

Manually confirm:

Pin 1 is VBUS
Pin 2 is D−
Pin 3 is D+
Pin 4 is GND
Shields are not accidentally treated as signal pins
D+ never crosses to D−
No power-output symbol drives VBUS
Every optional placeholder is clearly marked DNP where appropriate
Every ERC warning is fixed or documented
Why this matters

Success test

The session is complete when seleceted, any pin on the host connector and trace one clearly labelled path to the correct pin on the device connector, with no unexplained ERC errors.
