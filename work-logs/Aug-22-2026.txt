1. Record answers to:

-Why D+ & D- require a continuous ground reference


Route the high-speed USB signals on the plane closest to the ground plane whenever possible. Avoid running signal traces across a split in the
ground or power plane. Minimizing the number of signal vias reduces EMI by reducing inductance at high frequencies. the ground plane provides the high-frequency return current with a short, low-impedance path. A gap or split forces that current to detour, increasing loop area, EMI, noise and impedance variation.

Source: TI AM335x and AM43xx USB Layout Guidelines @ Section 2.7: Image Planes

-How branches, large test pads and vias create impedance discontinuities

These physical structures change the trace geometry:

A branch becomes an unterminated transmission-line stub that reflects part of the signal. A large test pad adds conductor area and therefore additional capacitance to the ground plane. A via adds inductance, capacitance and possibly an unused via stub.

Source: TI High-Speed Interface Layout Guidelines @ Section 3.6 Via Discontinuity Mitigation &  Section 3.8 Increase Via Anti-Pad Diameter

-Why ESD-device capacitance loads the data lines

An ESD diode is electrically connected from each data line toward ground. Even when it is not conducting an ESD pulse, its semiconductor junction has parasitic capacitance. That capacitance draws more high-frequency current as frequency. It can act as a frequency dependant shunt load

Source: Picking ESD Diodes for Ultra High-Speed Data Lines @ Section 4 Layout Considerations

-Why the ESD device belongs close to the exposed connector

The ESD device must intercept the discharge immediately after it enters through the connector. Placing it close minimizes the inductance between the connector.

Source: Picking ESD Diodes for Ultra High-Speed Data Lines @ Section 4 Layout Considerations

Update usb2-requirements.md containing these preliminary rules:

Target 90 Ω differential impedance; final geometry determined from the fabricator’s stack-up.
Keep D+/D− short, parallel and on one layer where practical.
Maintain constant width, spacing and ground reference.
Avoid routing over plane gaps.
Avoid unnecessary vias and branches.
Put test pads directly in the route, not at the end of long traces.
Use a two-channel ESD device explicitly rated for USB 2.0 High-Speed.
Route through or immediately beside the ESD device with a short ground connection.
